Recipely

Helping people transform everyday ingredients into delicious meals through AI.

Year 2026
Timeline 1 Week
Role UI/UX Designer
Platform iOS / Android

The Problem

Most recipe apps assume users already know what they want to cook. In reality, students and young professionals often open their fridge or pantry with one question: "What can I cook with what I already have?"

This uncertainty leads to food waste, impulse takeout spending, and decision fatigue. The goal of this project was to design an intuitive interface that bridges the gap between available ingredients and meal preparation — ensuring the experience is accessible, usable, and centered around real user needs.

My Role as UI/UX Designer

I was responsible for translating the core concept of an AI ingredient scanner into a usable, intuitive, and visually engaging mobile experience. My focus was on establishing user-centered flows, building wireframes grounded in accessibility principles, and creating a scalable design system for consistent, high-fidelity screens.

Tools: Figma, Notion, Draw.io

Type: Personal Project / Capstone Concept

Key Deliverables

End-to-End User Journeys Designed
100% Scalable Design System
70+ High-Fidelity Screens
AT A GLANCE

Cook Smarter, Waste Less

Recipely turns kitchen confusion into instant culinary ideas. The interface was designed to minimize manual input, prioritizing a camera-first workflow to eliminate the friction of searching for recipes — making cooking accessible for everyone from students to busy professionals.

01 / CHALLENGE

Where It All Started

The Spark

The concept for Recipely started in a friend's boarding house kitchen. Looking at a random assortment of leftover vegetables and half-used sauces, it became clear how much effort it took to search up a recipe that matched only what was on the counter.

We'd stare at the fridge, argue about what was cookable, and eventually just order takeout — again. That moment of helplessness in front of perfectly usable ingredients is what sparked the idea.

"Uncertainty in the kitchen leads to unnecessary food waste and impulse takeout spending."

UX Strategy & Logic

The UX Challenge The Interface Solution
Friction in data entry. Users don't want to type out 15 different ingredients. Camera-First Workflow: A prominent scanning UI that detects ingredients instantly — prioritizing accessibility and speed.
Information overload. Recipes have too many ingredients users don't own. Visual Match Indicators: UI tags that clearly rank recipes by "Ingredient Match %" — ensuring usability at a glance.
Losing track while cooking. Users touch dirty screens while trying to read small text. Focus Mode UI: A high-contrast, step-by-step interface tailored for active cooking — designed for accessibility in real kitchen conditions.
02 / DESIGN & EXECUTION

From Blueprint to Interface

Information Architecture & User Flow

Before jumping into high-fidelity screens, I mapped the complete user journey in FigJam to ensure the scanning-to-cooking pipeline required minimum friction. Every touchpoint was designed with user-centered principles — reducing cognitive load and ensuring intuitive navigation.

Home → Camera Scan → Ingredient Verification → Filtered Recipes → Cooking Mode

The Design System

To maintain visual consistency across 70+ screens, I built a lightweight, accessible UI kit ensuring every component followed the same design language:

  • Color Palette: Warm, food-inspired primary tones paired with high-contrast neutral backgrounds for readability in bright kitchen environments.
  • Typography: Clean sans-serif fonts optimized for legibility at a glance — meeting accessibility standards for varied lighting conditions.
  • Components: Reusable recipe cards, tag chips, button states, bottom navigation, and modal sheets — all built with Figma Auto-Layout for consistency and scalability.

High-Fidelity Execution

Each screen grouping was designed with a specific UX rationale — ensuring the interface is not just visually engaging, but genuinely intuitive and accessible for real users in real kitchens.

The AI Scanner

Designed to mimic native camera apps so users immediately know how to interact with it. The scanning interface prioritizes speed and clarity — detected ingredients appear as editable tags, giving users full control before generating matches.

Recipe Discovery

Structured using a card-based layout to make scanning for meals highly visual and appetizing. AI recommendations and community creations are clearly separated, ensuring users can find inspiration without cognitive overload.

Saved & Profile

A clean dashboard to organize favorites, past meals, and app settings. The profile section was designed with accessibility in mind — clear hierarchy, consistent spacing, and intuitive navigation patterns familiar to mobile users.

03 / REFLECTION

Looking Back & Moving Forward

What I Learned

Designing Recipely reinforced that effective UI/UX is about removing barriers. Grounding the interface in a real-world struggle — staring at leftover ingredients in a boarding house kitchen — helped me prioritize clarity over clutter and user needs over assumptions.

I also deepened my technical skills in Figma by building a strict, reusable component library that allowed me to scale the design to 70+ screens efficiently while maintaining visual consistency, accessibility standards, and user-centered design principles throughout every interaction.

If I Had More Time

If this project were to advance, I would focus on designing interfaces for:

  • AI Budget-Based Planning: UI dashboards tailoring meal suggestions based on local grocery market prices — making smart cooking accessible to budget-conscious users.
  • Pantry Expiration Tracking: Alert components and push notification logic for expiring items — reducing food waste through intuitive, timely nudges.
  • Voice-Guided Cooking: Hands-free UI states for the active cooking mode — extending accessibility to users who can't interact with screens while cooking.
